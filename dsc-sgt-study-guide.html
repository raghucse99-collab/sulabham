1. Can you crack it?

Yes, this is a realistic target. Kurnool has 213 SGT posts, the most of any district in Andhra Pradesh (statewide total is 1,205 SGT posts). The exam content is school-level (Classes 3 to 8).

Why it is still competitive: the paper is easy, so thousands of candidates score high. Selection is decided by a few marks. Your aim is not "know a lot" but "make almost zero mistakes".

Check these three things first (before 3 Nov 2026, when the Detailed Notification comes):

  • AP TET (Paper-I A) pass — SGT needs a TET qualification. TET marks also add weightage to your final score. If you have not passed TET, check whether a TET is scheduled before DSC.
  • Age limit and local status — DSC posts are district-level. Confirm your local district and age eligibility in the Detailed Notification.
  • Exam date — If you are writing other exams (like APPSC), plan both together (Section 3 shows how).

Advantage for APPSC aspirants: GK and Current Affairs (8 marks) plus most of Social Studies content overlap with Group 1/Group 2 preparation. The genuinely new parts are Educational Psychology, Perspectives in Education and Teaching Methodology (32 marks in total).

  • Final merit (as in the last DSC): written marks scaled to 80% + TET score weightage 20%. Confirm this in the Detailed Notification.

3. Preparation strategy and 10-week plan

Study DSC for 2 to 2.5 hours a day. Use this cycle: learn → write from memory → fill gaps → MCQ test.

Materials (keep it small):

  1. AP state textbooks, Classes 3 to 8 (2024-25) for Telugu, English, Maths, EVS/Science and Social. Download the PDFs from the AP School Education / SCERT website. This is your main book.
  2. One standard DSC Psychology + Perspectives guide (Telugu Academy or any well-known DSC publisher). Read the first 8 chapters properly.
  3. D.El.Ed methodology books you used in TTC. Methodology questions come straight from these ideas.
  4. Previous DSC SGT question papers (2018, 2025). Solve each one in 2.5 hours, then study every wrong answer.

10-week plan:

  1. Week 1-2: Psychology (Section 5) and Perspectives (Section 4). Classes 3-5 Maths and EVS textbooks.
  2. Week 3-4: Classes 6-8 Maths and Science. Telugu grammar (sandhi, samasalu, alankaralu, chandassu).
  3. Week 5-6: Classes 6-8 Social. English grammar (tenses, articles, prepositions, sentence types). Methodology for all five subjects (Section 6).
  4. Week 7-8: Revise everything once. 1 previous paper every 2 days.
  5. Week 9-10: Full mock tests only. Make a "mistakes notebook" and revise it daily.

Daily routine (2.5 hours):

  • 60 min: one textbook chapter, note only facts that can become a question.
  • 45 min: Psychology / Perspectives / Methodology.
  • 30 min: 40 MCQs and correction.
  • 15 min: Telugu or English grammar drill.

Key rules:

  • Read the textbook line by line. DSC asks questions from small boxes, tables and "Do you know?" notes.
  • For Maths, practise speed: you get roughly 1 minute per question.
  • For Psychology, learn who said what (psychologist → theory → key word). This alone gives 4 to 5 marks.

4. Perspectives in Education (4 marks)

This part asks "which committee, which year, what did it recommend". Learn the table below by heart; it covers most questions.

4.1 History of education

  • Vedic period: Gurukula system — student lived with the teacher; oral learning; aim was character and spiritual growth.
  • Buddhist period: Education in viharas and universities like Nalanda and Takshashila; open to all castes.
  • Medieval period: Maktabs (primary) and Madrasas (higher) for Islamic education.
Year Commission / Policy Remember it for

1882 Hunter Commission Primary education; handed it to local bodies
1929 Hartog Committee "Wastage and stagnation" in primary education
1944 Sargent Plan Free, compulsory education for ages 6-14 within 40 years
1952-53 Mudaliar Commission Secondary education; diversified courses
1964-66 Kothari Commission 10+2+3 system; common school system; "destiny of India is being shaped in her classrooms"
1977 Ishwarbhai Patel Committee SUPW — Socially Useful Productive Work
1986 National Policy on Education (NPE) Operation Blackboard; Navodaya Vidyalayas
1992 Programme of Action (POA) Revised NPE 1986

4.3 Acts and rights

  • RTE Act 2009 (in force 1 April 2010): free and compulsory education for ages 6-14. Based on Article 21A, added by the 86th Amendment (2002). 25% seats in private unaided schools for weaker sections. Pupil-teacher ratio 30:1 (primary), 35:1 (upper primary). The 2019 amendment allows states to hold back students in Classes 5 and 8.
  • RTI Act 2005: reply within 30 days; 48 hours if life or liberty is involved. Every office has a Public Information Officer (PIO).
  • Child rights: UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989), India ratified 1992. Four rights: survival, development, protection, participation. NCPCR protects child rights in India. POCSO Act 2012 protects children from sexual offences.
  • Human rights: Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 10 December 1948 (Human Rights Day). NHRC set up in 1993.
  • RPwD Act 2016: recognises 21 disabilities; basis of inclusive education.

4.4 NCF 2005 (National Curriculum Framework)

Prepared by NCERT under Prof. Yash Pal. Main idea: children construct knowledge (constructivism); the teacher is a facilitator. Its 5 guiding principles:

  1. Connect knowledge to life outside school.
  2. Move away from rote learning.
  3. Enrich the curriculum beyond textbooks.
  4. Make exams flexible and part of classroom life.
  5. Build an identity based on caring and democratic values.

4.5 NEP 2020 (National Education Policy)

Drafted by the committee under Dr. K. Kasturirangan; approved 29 July 2020.

  • 5+3+3+4 structure: Foundational (age 3-8), Preparatory (8-11), Middle (11-14), Secondary (14-18).
  • Foundational literacy and numeracy by Class 3 (NIPUN Bharat mission).
  • Mother tongue as medium till at least Class 5, preferably Class 8.
  • Vocational education from Class 6; PARAKH as national assessment centre.
  • Spending target: 6% of GDP on education. 100% school enrolment (GER) by 2030.
  • 4-year integrated B.Ed. as minimum teacher qualification by 2030.

4.6 Teacher empowerment and contemporary concerns

  • NCTE (National Council for Teacher Education) became a statutory body in 1995 — sets teacher education standards. SCERT at state level, DIET at district level train teachers.
  • Equality = same for all; Equity = more support for those who need it; Quality = learning actually happens.
  • Education as human capital: money spent on education is an investment that raises productivity.
  • Inclusive education: children with special needs study in the same classroom with support.

5. Educational Psychology (8 marks)

Psychology questions test whether you can match a psychologist to an idea and apply it to a classroom situation. Learn each theory as: who → what → classroom use.

5.1 Growth, development, maturation

  • Growth (పెరుగుదల): physical increase in size — height, weight. Measurable; stops at a point.
  • Development (వికాసం): overall change in body, mind, emotions and behaviour. Continues for life.
  • Maturation (పరిపక్వత): natural unfolding from inside, without training. Example: a baby walks when its muscles are ready.

Principles of development: continuous; follows a fixed order; cephalocaudal (head to toe — a baby controls its head before its legs); proximodistal (centre to outside — shoulder before fingers); general to specific; individuals differ; result of heredity × environment.

Stages: Infancy (0-2), Early childhood (2-6), Later childhood (6-12), Adolescence (12-18). SGT teaches later childhood, so its features are asked often: group feeling ("gang age"), curiosity, concrete thinking.

5.2 Theories of development (most asked)

Piaget — cognitive development. A child builds mental "schemas". New information is taken into an existing schema (assimilation) or the schema is changed (accommodation); balance between them is equilibration.

Exam favourite: "conservation" (same quantity in a different shape) appears in the concrete operational stage; "egocentrism" belongs to the pre-operational stage.

Vygotsky — social learning of the mind. Learning happens through interaction. ZPD (Zone of Proximal Development) = what a child can do with help but not alone. Scaffolding = temporary support given by the teacher, removed slowly.

Kohlberg — moral development. 3 levels, 6 stages, studied with the "Heinz dilemma": Pre-conventional (avoid punishment, get reward) → Conventional (be a "good child", obey law) → Post-conventional (social contract, universal principles).

Erikson — psychosocial development. 8 stages, each a conflict. For school: Initiative vs Guilt (3-6), Industry vs Inferiority (6-12), Identity vs Role confusion (12-18).

Chomsky — language. Every child is born with a Language Acquisition Device (LAD).

Carl Rogers — self theory. Child-centred, non-directive counselling.

5.3 Individual differences and intelligence

Psychologist Theory Key point
Spearman Two-factor theory General factor (g) + specific factors (s)
Thorndike Multi-factor theory Intelligence = many separate abilities
Thurstone Primary Mental Abilities 7 abilities
Guilford Structure of Intellect 180 abilities; divergent thinking = creativity
Howard Gardner Multiple Intelligences 8 types: linguistic, logical, spatial, musical, bodily, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalist
Sternberg Triarchic theory Analytical, creative, practical
  • IQ = (Mental Age ÷ Chronological Age) × 100. IQ term by William Stern. Binet made the first intelligence test.
  • Example: MA 12, CA 10 → IQ = 120.
  • EQ (emotional intelligence): popularised by Daniel Goleman.
  • Creativity stages (Wallas): preparation → incubation → illumination → verification.

5.4 Learning theories

Psychologist Theory Experiment Classroom use
Thorndike Trial and error Cat in a puzzle box Laws of readiness, exercise, effect — practice and reward
Pavlov Classical conditioning Dog, bell and food Building habits, removing fear
Skinner Operant conditioning Rat / pigeon in Skinner box Reinforcement; programmed instruction
Kohler Insight learning Chimpanzee Sultan and sticks Understanding the whole problem; "aha" moment
Bandura Observational / social learning Bobo doll Teacher as a model
Bruner Discovery learning — Enactive → iconic → symbolic learning
  • Reinforcement: positive (give something pleasant) and negative (remove something unpleasant). Both increase behaviour. Punishment decreases it.
  • Learning curve plateau: a flat period where progress seems to stop.
  • Transfer of learning: positive (cycling helps motorbike), negative (one habit blocks another), zero.
  • Memory and forgetting: Ebbinghaus forgetting curve — we forget fastest just after learning. Spaced revision fixes this.
  • Maslow's needs: physiological → safety → love → esteem → self-actualisation. A hungry child cannot learn well.

5.5 Personality

  • Freud: Id (desires), Ego (reality), Superego (conscience).
  • Jung: introvert, extrovert, ambivert.
  • Allport: trait theory.
  • Projective tests: Rorschach ink-blot test; TAT (Thematic Apperception Test, Murray and Morgan).
  • Defence mechanisms: rationalisation (excuse), projection (blaming others), repression (forgetting painful memories), sublimation (turning energy into useful work).

5.6 Children with special needs

Inclusive education: CWSN learn in regular classrooms with support. Common learning disabilities: dyslexia (reading), dysgraphia (writing), dyscalculia (maths).

6. Teaching Methodology (20 marks across 5 subjects)

Every subject's methodology repeats the same ideas: aims and objectives, methods, teaching aids, lesson plan, evaluation. Learn these once and apply them to all five subjects.

6.1 Aims vs objectives and Bloom's taxonomy

  • Aim = broad, long-term goal (e.g. "develop scientific attitude"). Objective = specific, measurable, for one lesson.
  • Bloom's taxonomy (1956), cognitive domain: Knowledge → Comprehension → Application → Analysis → Synthesis → Evaluation.
  • Revised (Anderson, 2001): Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → Create.
  • Three domains: cognitive (thinking), affective (feelings, attitudes — Krathwohl), psychomotor (skills — Simpson/Dave).

6.2 Maxims of teaching

Known → unknown; simple → complex; concrete → abstract; particular → general; whole → part; observation → reasoning.

6.3 Teaching methods

Method Given by / idea Best for
Inductive Examples → rule Maths formulas, grammar rules (best for young children)
Deductive Rule → examples Quick application, practice
Analytic Unknown → known (breaking down) Geometry proofs
Synthetic Known → unknown (building up) Writing the proof neatly
Project method Kilpatrick (based on Dewey) Real-life purposeful tasks
Heuristic method H.E. Armstrong Child as discoverer — science
Play-way method Froebel (Kindergarten) Early classes
Montessori method Maria Montessori Sense training, self-learning
Laboratory / experiment Learning by doing Science
Source method Using original sources Social studies
  • Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience: direct, real experiences (bottom) give the best learning; verbal symbols (top) are the most abstract.
  • Teaching-learning material (TLM): should be low-cost, local, safe, and suitable for the child's age.

6.4 Lesson planning

  • Year plan → Unit plan → Lesson (period) plan.
  • Herbart's 5 steps: Preparation → Presentation → Association (comparison) → Generalisation → Application.

6.5 Evaluation and CCE

  • CCE = Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation. Continuous = throughout the year; comprehensive = covers both scholastic and co-scholastic areas.
  • Formative assessment (during learning — observation, projects, written work) vs Summative assessment (at the end of a term).
  • Diagnostic test finds the learning difficulty → remedial teaching fixes it.
  • Achievement test measures what was learned; a good test is valid (measures what it should) and reliable (gives consistent results).

6.6 Subject-specific points

  • Telugu: four skills — వినడం, మాట్లాడడం, చదవడం, రాయడం. Teach poems with expression and recitation; prose by reading and discussion.
  • English: LSRW (Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing). Methods: Grammar-Translation (oldest; uses mother tongue), Direct method (no mother tongue), Structural approach (sentence patterns), Communicative approach (language for real use), Bilingual method (C.J. Dodson). English is taught as a second language.
  • Maths: abstract subject, so go from concrete objects to symbols. Use inductive method for formulas. Remedial teaching for common errors.
  • Science: develop scientific attitude and process skills (observe, classify, hypothesise, experiment). Science fairs, clubs, field trips, museums.
  • Social: develop citizenship and democratic values. Use maps, globes, timelines, field trips and the source method.

7. Content subjects: what to focus on (40 marks)

Each subject gives 8 content marks from Classes 3-8 textbooks. Focus on the topics below; they come up most in the syllabus.

Subject High-priority topics
Telugu Kavi parichayalu (poets and their works), padajalam (synonyms, antonyms, nanarthalu, vyutpattyarthalu), sandhulu, samasalu, chandassu (gana vibhajana), alankaralu, vakyalu
English Tenses, articles, prepositions, parts of speech, types of sentences, poets/writers in textbooks, letter and essay formats

How to read a textbook for DSC:

  1. Read one chapter fully, including boxes, pictures and their captions.
  2. Write 10 one-line facts from it in a notebook.
  3. Next day, cover the notebook and recall the 10 facts.
  4. Solve the chapter's exercise questions orally.

Quick formulas to memorise for Maths:

  • Simple Interest = P × R × T ÷ 100
  • Compound amount = P(1 + R/100)n
  • Profit % = Profit ÷ Cost Price × 100
  • Area of triangle = ½ × base × height; circle = πr²; circumference = 2πr
  • Volume of cuboid = l × b × h; cylinder = πr²h
  • HCF × LCM = product of two numbers
  • Euler's formula (3D shapes): Faces + Vertices − Edges = 2
