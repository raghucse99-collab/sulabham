Paper II syllabus — 10 units in simple words

Paper I (GS) covers current affairs, science, Indian & AP history and geography, polity, economy, environment, disaster management, reasoning and data analysis.

How to prepare · ఎలా చదవాలి

  1. Apply before 2 Nov. Check your degree memo shows Statistics as a subject or paper. Keep study certificates (Classes 4–10) ready for local status — residence certificates are not accepted.
  2. Statistics first (units 6–10). Half of Paper II, and it is all numerical. Learn each formula with one worked example, then solve 20 problems per topic. Intermediate and degree statistics textbooks are enough.
  3. Economics next (units 1–3). Overlaps with Group 1 and 2 economy. Add the latest Union and AP budget figures.
  4. Accounting and computers (units 4–5). Short and scoring: practise journal entries, trial balance and number-system conversions until they are quick.
  5. Paper I daily. 1 hour of GS + reasoning. Use this site's monthly current affairs.
  6. Computer test. Practise MS Word, Excel and PowerPoint tasks and typing for 20 minutes a day from now — it is qualifying, but failing it means losing the job.

    Easy Explainers

    Tap a topic to open it. Read the steps in order: the meaning first, then the example, then the exam point.

    Repo Rate

    రెపో రేటు — RBI బ్యాంకులకు ఇచ్చే అప్పుపై వడ్డీ
    +
    1

    What is it?The interest rate at which RBI lends money to banks for a short time.

    2

    Simple exampleThink of RBI as the wholesale shop and banks as retail shops. If the wholesale price goes up, retail prices go up too. So when repo rate rises, home and vehicle loans become costlier.

    3

    Why does RBI do it?To control inflation. Less borrowing means less spending, so prices cool down. To boost the economy, RBI cuts the repo rate instead.

    Exam point: Repo rate is decided by the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), which has 6 members. Reverse repo is the opposite: the rate RBI pays banks for parking money with it.

    Article 371D

    ఆర్టికల్ 371D — ఉద్యోగాలు, విద్యలో స్థానికులకు రక్షణ
    +
    1

    What is it?A special provision in the Constitution that protects local people of Andhra Pradesh (and Telangana) in government jobs and education.

    2

    Simple exampleWhen you apply for a district-level post, candidates from that local zone get priority. That "local candidate" rule comes from Article 371D.

    3

    Why was it made?After the Mulki and Jai Andhra agitations, a fair system was needed so that every region got its share of jobs and seats.

    Exam point: Added by the 32nd Constitutional Amendment in 1973. It also allows an Administrative Tribunal for service matters.

    Polavaram Project

    పోలవరం ప్రాజెక్టు — గోదావరిపై జాతీయ ప్రాజెక్టు
    +
    1

    What is it?A large multipurpose dam on the Godavari river for irrigation, drinking water and power.

    2

    Simple exampleGodavari has extra water that flows into the sea. Polavaram stores it and sends some towards the Krishna basin, so dry areas also get water.

    3

    Why is it "national"?Because the Centre declared it a national project, it funds the irrigation component.

    Exam point: Declared a national project under Section 90 of the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014.

    AP Reorganisation Act, 2014

    ఏపీ పునర్వ్యవస్థీకరణ చట్టం — రాష్ట్ర విభజన చట్టం
    +
    1

    What is it?The law that divided the old Andhra Pradesh into two states: Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

    2

    Simple exampleLike dividing a joint family property, it decided who gets which assets, employees, rivers' water and debts.

    3

    Key promisesPolavaram as a national project, central institutions for AP, and special support for backward districts.

    Exam point: Appointed day was 2 June 2014. Hyderabad was the common capital for up to 10 years.
